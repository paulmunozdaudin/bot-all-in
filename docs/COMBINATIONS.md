# Combination Engine

## 1. Purpose

Find statistically coherent multi-selection combinations — never "pick N
favorites" (brief Section 10). The engine can, and is expected to,
legitimately conclude "no combination meets the bar," which is a correct
output, not a bug (Sections 11, 22).

## 2. Step 1 — score each individual selection

For every candidate selection (a market outcome from the prediction
pipeline), the engine reads, from `MODEL.md`/`MARKET_ENGINE.md` output:

- model probability (calibrated)
- market probability (normalized)
- edge and EV
- confidence (model agreement / data quality score)
- historical performance in this bucket (from the backtest report)
- uncertainty (e.g., width of a probability interval from ensemble spread
  or Monte Carlo simulation — see §5)

## 3. Step 2 — correlation, not independence

This is the critical part the brief calls out explicitly (Section 10.2):
**never assume `P(A ∩ B) = P(A) × P(B)`** for selections that can be
correlated.

`backend/app/services/combination_engine/correlation.py` implements:

- **Same-match correlation via the joint model**: when selections A and B
  come from the same match, their joint probability is read directly from
  the Dixon-Coles score-matrix (or a Monte Carlo simulation of it, §5) that
  already produced both markets — e.g. "Home win" and "Over 2.5" are
  evaluated as one joint event over the score distribution, not multiplied
  as if independent. This falls directly out of `MODEL.md` §7's design
  choice to derive every market from one joint distribution per match.
- **Derived-market detection**: markets that are deterministic or
  near-deterministic functions of each other (e.g. "Home Win" and "Double
  Chance 1X") are flagged as **incompatible/redundant** and excluded from
  appearing together in a combination — combining them isn't extra edge,
  it's the same bet counted twice.
- **Cross-match correlation**: estimated from historical co-movement of
  outcomes across matches that share a plausible causal link (e.g., two
  matches in the same competition round affecting a team's rotation
  decisions) — modeled only where backtesting shows a non-negligible
  correlation exists; defaults to independence otherwise, but this default
  is stated explicitly in the output, not silently assumed.
- **Concentration risk**: flags combinations that repeatedly lean on the
  same team, league, or underlying factor (e.g., three selections that all
  depend on the same team's striker being fit) as correlated risk even
  when no formal correlation coefficient is modeled for that pair.

## 4. Step 3 — joint probability

When dependence is significant, joint probability is computed either
analytically (from the shared Dixon-Coles/Poisson joint distribution for
same-match combinations) or via Monte Carlo simulation (`MODEL.md`/§5 below)
for combinations spanning models without a closed-form joint distribution.
Only when selections are shown, empirically, to be effectively independent
(no detected correlation above a validated threshold) does the engine fall
back to the product rule — and that fallback is logged as such in the
combination's output, not hidden.

## 5. Monte Carlo simulation

`backend/app/models_stat/monte_carlo.py` simulates match scorelines (and
derived markets) by drawing from the fitted Dixon-Coles distribution N times
(default 10,000), which naturally preserves same-match correlation because
every simulated match draws one home-goals/away-goals pair per iteration,
and every market for that match is evaluated against the same draw. This is
the mechanism behind combined markets like "Home Win & Over 2.5 & BTTS" for
a single match, and behind uncertainty intervals (the spread of simulated
outcomes) fed into the confidence scores in §2.

## 6. Step 4 — risk profiles

Three profiles, generated by constrained search over eligible selections
(not hand-picked):

- **Conservative** — fewer selections, optimizes for higher combined
  probability subject to a minimum EV floor.
- **Balanced** — optimizes a blended objective of EV and combined
  probability.
- **High variance** — allows lower combined probability in exchange for
  higher EV/edge, subject to correlation and concentration-risk caps.

These labels describe a statistical profile of the search result, never a
guarantee (brief Section 10, closing note).

## 7. Ranking (brief Section 11)

Combinations are ranked by a documented, versioned scoring function
combining: Expected Value, combined probability, model agreement,
historical calibration in the relevant buckets, correlation penalty,
uncertainty, and (once market-liquidity data exists) a liquidity factor.
The scoring function's weights are fit/validated the same way model
thresholds are (`MARKET_ENGINE.md` §3) — against backtested combination
outcomes, not chosen by feel.

Hard caps prevent absurdly large combinations (a configurable max selection
count, default small, e.g. 4) regardless of how good individual EVs look,
because per-selection uncertainty compounds multiplicatively in a way the
correlation model above is explicitly trying to keep honest, not exploit.

## 8. Explicit "no result" behavior

If, after search, no combination clears the minimum bar (EV floor,
calibration requirement, correlation/concentration caps, sample-size
requirement), the API returns an explicit `NO_STRONG_COMBINATION_FOUND`
result with the reason, rather than relaxing filters to force a result
(brief Section 22). This is tested directly in
`backend/tests/test_combination_engine.py` with a fixture set designed so
that no combination should pass.
